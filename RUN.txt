NEUROREEF 2 - how to run

1. Double-click Neuroreef.exe.
   Windows may say "Windows protected your PC" because the program is not signed:
   click "More info", then "Run anyway".
2. A console window opens and your browser opens the Neuroreef console at http://localhost:5800
   Keep the console window open; closing it stops the simulation.
3. Tablet: the console window prints a line "on your network: http://192.168.x.x:5800".
   Open that address in the tablet's browser (same Wi-Fi). If Windows asks whether to allow
   Neuroreef on private networks, click Allow.

The world you watch is births and deaths only: no animal is ever spawned into it, and an extinct
species stays extinct. Its first animals need brains that can keep them alive. Those come from a
PREHISTORY, a separate run that stands for the long evolution before the world begins.

THE DEFAULTS (and what they need)
  The map is the supercontinent: 8 km across (2048 x 2048 cells of 16 m), with one prehistory branch.
  That takes about 4 GB of memory on a 16 GB PC. The animal slots a run starts with follow your memory
  (a quarter of it; about 19,000 slots on a 16 GB PC) and grow as births need more, as long as memory
  allows; the console window prints them at start, e.g.
    memory: the world starts with 19,456 slots x 213 KB = 4.0 GB; 15.9 GB available, 2.0 GB kept free; ...
  A status line every 10 seconds shows the step, steps/s (0 when nothing moved), PAUSED when paused, and
  the slots. If it says WORLD FULL, memory (or --max-capacity) and not food decided who was born.
  On a smaller PC choose "Light (4 km)" or a smaller map on the setup page.

The prehistory (the first start):
  With no evolved brains yet, the console opens on the Prehistory tab. One branch (by default; the setup
  page can add more) evolves at full speed: 500,000 steps, shown as 500 million symbolic years. Tap a
  branch to watch it in 3D; the
  coloured squares are the species (green = lives on its own, yellow = breeding, orange = dying, no births,
  red = gone). The sliders change mutation while it runs. "Begin the world now" ends it
  early. At the end the best brains of every species are saved as evolved.nrbank and the world begins:
  births and deaths only. The next start skips the prehistory; Neuroreef.exe --prehistory runs a new one.
  Closing the window during the prehistory saves prehistory-partial.nrbank.

  Evolve.bat runs the same prehistory without graphics and also writes evolve-report.txt (what
  happened to each species in each branch, and why). For a run of days or weeks use LongRun.bat
  (below): it saves itself, resumes after a restart and restarts itself after a crash.

YOUR WORLD IS SAVED
  The world saves itself every 30 minutes and when you close the window (Ctrl+C), to world.nrck.
  Starting Neuroreef.exe again continues it exactly where it was. To start a new world instead:
  Neuroreef.exe --new-world (the old one is kept as world.nrck.old).
  A save made by an older version of the program is continued too (and so is a prehistory in progress,
  prehistory.nrck): its animals, their genomes and brains, the species and the step are kept, and what the
  new version builds differently (the land, water, plants, disease in the ground) is rebuilt from the
  world's own seed and nature. The program prints what it kept and what it rebuilt.

SETUP BEFORE A NEW PREHISTORY
  When a new prehistory is about to start, the browser first shows a setup page: how many branches,
  animal slots per branch, founders per ancestor, its length, the big-brained ancestors, and the world's
  slots; the map (valley 1 km, region 2 km, continent 4 km or supercontinent 8 km; the world you watch can
  be smaller than the prehistory's map); the body sizes; and how hard nature is (plants, seasons, droughts,
  fire, disease, thirst, water and seasonal ponds; 1x is the documented law). It shows how much memory that
  takes against what your computer has. Nothing is created until you press Start. (Skip it with
  --no-setup; any of --branches, --branch-slots, --founders, --big-brains on the command line also skip it.)
  Presets:
    Pet valley        1 km valley, two branches, small and fast: to watch a few animals up close.
    Light (4 km)      a continent, 4 km across, one branch, about 3-3.6 GB: for a laptop or an 8 GB PC.
                      It kept 7-12 species in the size tests, but crowds them (2-11x the density of real
                      animals of their size).
    Normal (8 km)     the default: the supercontinent, one branch, about 4 GB. The smallest map that kept
                      7-13 species at about the density of real animals.
    Region, Continent, Supercontinent   bigger runs (more branches or slots; up to ~7 GB).
  Body sizes (the Bodies group; or --bodies normal|giants|tiny on the command line):
    Normal   0.1-40 kg (the default).
    Giants   100 kg-20 t. Real animals that big live at low density, so a giant world holds few of them;
             the page warns when the map is too small for them. Use a big map.
    Tiny     1 g-1 kg. Many small animals; the smallest (under ~2 g) are rare at the start because even
             the smallest brain costs them a large share of their energy.
  The four size classes' floors are in the same proportions of the range by default and can be set one by
  one (--body-floors A,B,C,D in kg).

SPEED
  The speed buttons are steps per second: 1/s, 3/s, 10/s, 30/s and max. They work in the prehistory and in
  the world. The prehistory runs flat out; the world starts at the speed set in setup (3/s). If the computer cannot keep up, it runs as fast as it can (the "steps/s" box shows the real speed).

DATA EXPORT
  The "Export data" button (top bar) downloads everything recorded so far as a zip: population.csv
  (alive per clade over time), traits.csv, deaths.csv, clades.csv, telemetry.csv (every number) and
  report.txt. The same files are also written to the exports folder beside Neuroreef.exe every 30
  minutes and when you close it. At the end of a prehistory, every branch's data is exported too.

BIG-BRAINED ANCESTORS AND THE MATING INSTINCT
  Every sexual animal is born with a mating instinct (strongest in males). In each prehistory branch,
  1 of the 16 ancestors (10 on land, 6 in water) starts with 256 brain units; the others start with up to 24.
  A bigger brain costs more energy; whether it pays is up to evolution. To change the experiment:
  Neuroreef.exe --prehistory --big-brains 2 --big-brain-units 128     (or LongRun.bat: edit its line)
  --big-brains 0 gives every ancestor an ordinary brain.

BRANCHES
  At the end of a prehistory every branch's brains are saved: branch-0.nrbank, branch-1.nrbank, ...
  On the Prehistory tab, "Begin the world from branch N" picks the branch your world starts from.
  To start a world from another branch later: Neuroreef.exe --new-world --load-bank branch-1.nrbank

RUNNING THE PREHISTORY FOR WEEKS (LongRun.bat)
  Double-click LongRun.bat. It evolves with no graphics, so the whole CPU goes to evolution, and runs
  until you stop it. This is the way to do any long run. It uses the defaults (8 km, one branch, about
  4 GB of memory). Every 30 minutes it saves its whole state to prehistory.nrck (a few GB).
  - If the PC restarts, the power goes or the window is closed: double-click LongRun.bat again. It
    resumes from the last save; at most 30 minutes are lost. It also restarts itself after a crash.
  - To see how it is doing: the window prints a line every 10,000 steps (alive organisms and
    self-sustaining species per branch). Press Ctrl+C, then double-click LongRun.bat again: it saves
    the report evolve-report.txt and the brains evolved.nrbank, then carries on from where it was.
  - To stop for good: press Ctrl+C. Then double-click Neuroreef.exe to watch the world that evolved.
    (Rename prehistory.nrck first if you also want Neuroreef.exe to stop offering to resume it.)
  Before you leave it running:
  - Plug in the power. LongRun.bat turns off sleep and hibernate while on mains power.
  - Settings > Windows Update > Pause updates (up to 5 weeks), so Windows does not restart the PC.
  - Optional: to start it automatically after a restart, press Win+R, type shell:startup, and put a
    shortcut to LongRun.bat in the folder that opens.
  - Keep about 10 GB free on the drive (the state file and its temporary copy while saving).
  Start it again with the same options it was started with (LongRun.bat always uses the same ones).

  Neuroreef.exe (the console) also saves its prehistory every 30 minutes and offers to resume it,
  if you prefer to watch the branches in 3D while they evolve (slower: the graphics take CPU).

Options (run from a command prompt in this folder):
  Neuroreef.exe --random-brains      open the world with random brains even if evolved.nrbank exists
  Neuroreef.exe --evolve             what Evolve.bat runs
  Neuroreef.exe --seed 7             a different world and run
  Neuroreef.exe --save-bank my.nrbank   save evolved genomes every 50,000 steps and on exit
  Neuroreef.exe --load-bank my.nrbank   continue from saved genomes
  Neuroreef.exe --headless --steps 200000 --csv run.csv   long run without the console
  Neuroreef.exe --help               all options

BODY LAB (the body-plan prototype, docs/BODYPLAN.md)
  A separate experiment, not the world: bodies start as one soft blob and evolve a growing list of
  segments and appendages with no labelled organs; physics alone decides what each part does. It does
  not touch your world or prehistory and saves nothing but its own files. Double-click BodyLab.bat, or
  from a command prompt in this folder:
    Neuroreef.exe --bodylab                                   all three environments, seed 1, 800 generations
    Neuroreef.exe --bodylab --env sea-floor --seeds 3          seeds 1, 2 and 3 on the sea floor
    Neuroreef.exe --bodylab --env land --seed 7 --generations 400 --population 150
  --env open-water | sea-floor | land | all     --seeds N (from --seed, default 1) or a list: --seeds 3,7,9
  --generations N (default 800)   --population N (default 200)   --every N (a snapshot every N generations)
  --out FOLDER (default: the bodylab folder next to the exe)   --no-serve (run, write the files, exit)
  A run of 800 generations takes seconds to a minute. The window prints a line per batch of generations
  (best fitness, species, the champion's mass, segments, appendage pairs, how it moves, rigid or soft),
  then a table like the prototype's demo report and a full account of each run's best body.
  Each run is written to bodylab\bodylab-<env>-<seed>.json: the champion every few generations, the best
  of each species, and what the physics says each part does.
  Your browser opens the Body Lab page at http://localhost:5801/bodylab.html, so it can run beside an open world on 5800 (if 5801 is taken it uses the next free port and prints it; --port N, --no-browser):
  - the champion turns slowly in 3D (drag to orbit, wheel to zoom, double-click to frame it again);
  - the timeline under it goes from generation 1, the blob, to the last; press Play to watch it change;
  - the strip below shows the best body of each species at that generation, side by side;
  - the right panel says what each part does, from the physics' numbers (thrust, support, crushing,
    gas exchange, protection). Click a part there to move the camera to it; tick "role colours" to
    colour every part by its role. Tissue colour: pink soft skin, amber cuticle, ivory mineral; a shiny
    shell over the back is a rigid integument; red fringes are gills; dark openings are folded-in
    exchange (lungs, tracheae); knuckles on an appendage are its joints.
  - "Run live" evolves a new run on the page while you watch (one at a time).
  The window keeps serving the page until you press Ctrl+C. The page also opens from the normal console
  ("body lab" in its top bar): there it shows bodylab-*.json files you open with "open file...".

ECOLOGY REPORT (NR_ECO=1)
  A headless run with the environment variable NR_ECO=1 also prints an ecology census every 300 steps
  (animals and biomass by trophic level, plant production and how much of it is eaten, deaths by cause,
  disease) and at the end, for each species, its density against real animals of its size (Damuth's
  rule), its life table and causes of death. It only reads the world; the run is the same without it.
  In PowerShell, in this folder:
    $env:NR_ECO="1"; .\Neuroreef.exe --headless --random-brains --map valley --seed 7 --steps 2000 --report 300 --threads 4
  (--map valley: the 1 km valley, quick; --random-brains: no prehistory needed; --report 300: a report line
  every 300 steps, with the census.) The totals over the run ("ECO POOLED") start after the first year,
  3,000 steps; for a shorter run like this one also set $env:NR_ECO_FROM="300". The variables stay set in
  that PowerShell window; Remove-Item Env:NR_ECO closes the census. In a command prompt (cmd):
    set NR_ECO=1
    Neuroreef.exe --headless --random-brains --map valley --seed 7 --steps 2000 --report 300 --threads 4
