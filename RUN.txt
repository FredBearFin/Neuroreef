NEUROREEF 2 - how to run

1. Double-click Neuroreef.exe.
   Windows may say "Windows protected your PC" because the program is not signed:
   click "More info", then "Run anyway".
2. A console window opens and your browser opens the Neuroreef console at http://localhost:5800
   Keep the console window open; closing it stops the simulation.
3. Tablet: the console window prints a line "on your network: http://192.168.x.x:5800".
   Open that address in the tablet's browser (same Wi-Fi). If Windows asks whether to allow
   Neuroreef on private networks, click Allow.

The world you watch is births and deaths only: no animal is ever spawned into it, and an extinct
species stays extinct. Its first animals need brains that can keep them alive. Those come from a
PREHISTORY, a separate run that stands for the long evolution before the world begins.

The prehistory (the first start):
  With no evolved brains yet, the console opens on the Prehistory tab. Three branches evolve at full
  speed: 500,000 steps, shown as 500 million symbolic years. Tap a branch to watch it in 3D; the
  coloured squares are the species (green = lives on its own, yellow = breeding, orange = dying, no births,
  red = gone). The sliders change mutation while it runs. "Begin the world now" ends it
  early. At the end the best brains of every species are saved as evolved.nrbank and the world begins:
  births and deaths only. The next start skips the prehistory; Neuroreef.exe --prehistory runs a new one.
  Closing the window during the prehistory saves prehistory-partial.nrbank.

  Evolve.bat runs the same prehistory without graphics and also writes evolve-report.txt (what
  happened to each species in each branch, and why).

YOUR WORLD IS SAVED
  The world saves itself every 30 minutes and when you close the window (Ctrl+C), to world.nrck.
  Starting Neuroreef.exe again continues it exactly where it was. To start a new world instead:
  Neuroreef.exe --new-world (the old one is kept as world.nrck.old).
  A save made by an older version of the program is continued too (and so is a prehistory in progress,
  prehistory.nrck): its animals, their genomes and brains, the species and the step are kept, and what the
  new version builds differently (the land, water, plants, disease in the ground) is rebuilt from the
  world's own seed and nature. The program prints what it kept and what it rebuilt.

SETUP BEFORE A NEW PREHISTORY
  When a new prehistory is about to start, the browser first shows a setup page: how many branches,
  animal slots per branch, founders per ancestor, its length, the big-brained ancestors, and the world's
  slots; the map (valley, region or continent; the world you watch can be a smaller valley than the
  prehistory's map); and how hard nature is (plants, seasons, droughts, fire, disease, thirst, water and
  seasonal ponds; 1x is the documented law). It shows how much memory that takes against what your computer
  has. Presets: Pet valley, Light (laptop), Normal, Big. Nothing is created until you press Start. (Skip it with --no-setup; any of --branches,
  --branch-slots, --founders, --big-brains on the command line also skip it.)

SPEED
  The speed buttons are steps per second: 1/s, 3/s, 10/s, 30/s and max. They work in the prehistory and in
  the world. The prehistory runs flat out; the world starts at the speed set in setup (3/s). If the computer cannot keep up, it runs as fast as it can (the "steps/s" box shows the real speed).

DATA EXPORT
  The "Export data" button (top bar) downloads everything recorded so far as a zip: population.csv
  (alive per clade over time), traits.csv, deaths.csv, clades.csv, telemetry.csv (every number) and
  report.txt. The same files are also written to the exports folder beside Neuroreef.exe every 30
  minutes and when you close it. At the end of a prehistory, every branch's data is exported too.

BIG-BRAINED ANCESTORS AND THE MATING INSTINCT
  Every sexual animal is born with a mating instinct (strongest in males). In each prehistory branch,
  2 of the 10 ancestors (one on land, one in water) start with 36 brain units, three times the others.
  A bigger brain costs more energy; whether it pays is up to evolution. To change the experiment:
  Neuroreef.exe --prehistory --big-brains 4 --big-brain-units 24     (or LongRun.bat: edit its line)
  --big-brains 0 gives every ancestor an ordinary brain.

BRANCHES
  At the end of a prehistory every branch's brains are saved: branch-0.nrbank, branch-1.nrbank, ...
  On the Prehistory tab, "Begin the world from branch N" picks the branch your world starts from.
  To start a world from another branch later: Neuroreef.exe --new-world --load-bank branch-1.nrbank

RUNNING THE PREHISTORY FOR WEEKS (LongRun.bat)
  Double-click LongRun.bat. It evolves with no graphics, so the whole CPU goes to evolution, and runs
  until you stop it. Every 30 minutes it saves its whole state to prehistory.nrck (about 2-3 GB).
  - If the PC restarts, the power goes or the window is closed: double-click LongRun.bat again. It
    resumes from the last save; at most 30 minutes are lost. It also restarts itself after a crash.
  - To see how it is doing: the window prints a line every 10,000 steps (alive organisms and
    self-sustaining species per branch). Press Ctrl+C, then double-click LongRun.bat again: it saves
    the report evolve-report.txt and the brains evolved.nrbank, then carries on from where it was.
  - To stop for good: press Ctrl+C. Then double-click Neuroreef.exe to watch the world that evolved.
    (Rename prehistory.nrck first if you also want Neuroreef.exe to stop offering to resume it.)
  Before you leave it running:
  - Plug in the power. LongRun.bat turns off sleep and hibernate while on mains power.
  - Settings > Windows Update > Pause updates (up to 5 weeks), so Windows does not restart the PC.
  - Optional: to start it automatically after a restart, press Win+R, type shell:startup, and put a
    shortcut to LongRun.bat in the folder that opens.
  - Keep about 10 GB free on the drive (the state file and its temporary copy while saving).
  Start it again with the same options it was started with (LongRun.bat always uses the same ones).

  Neuroreef.exe (the console) also saves its prehistory every 30 minutes and offers to resume it,
  if you prefer to watch the branches in 3D while they evolve (slower: the graphics take CPU).

Options (run from a command prompt in this folder):
  Neuroreef.exe --random-brains      open the world with random brains even if evolved.nrbank exists
  Neuroreef.exe --evolve             what Evolve.bat runs
  Neuroreef.exe --seed 7             a different world and run
  Neuroreef.exe --save-bank my.nrbank   save evolved genomes every 50,000 steps and on exit
  Neuroreef.exe --load-bank my.nrbank   continue from saved genomes
  Neuroreef.exe --headless --steps 200000 --csv run.csv   long run without the console
  Neuroreef.exe --help               all options
